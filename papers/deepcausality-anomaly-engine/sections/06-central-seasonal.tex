\section{The Central Seasonal Tier}
\label{sec:seasonal}

The edge engine is small and stateless. That is what makes it fast, and it is also
what limits what it can detect. A window of a few hundred samples is too short to
see seasonality. At nine in the morning that window still holds the overnight-quiet
samples, so the normal morning ramp trips a breach even though it happens every
weekday, and the window has no way to know the ramp is routine. Telling a real
problem from an ordinary Tuesday morning takes weeks of history, and the agent does
not keep weeks of history. The core does, in its aggregated metric store, so the
seasonal check belongs there and not on the agent.

The core compares each series against its own past at the same point in the week.
For a series it reads the aggregated history from the last several weeks and
computes, for each hour-of-week bucket, a mean and a standard deviation. It then
takes the most recent completed bucket and measures its \emph{deseasonalized
residual}: how far the value lands from that bucket's mean, in units of the
bucket's standard deviation. Cross the threshold and the series is off its normal
for this hour of the week, even when the raw value looks unremarkable to the edge's
short window. The case the edge cannot catch is a host that sits at twice its usual
load all Tuesday morning without ever spiking: nothing trips the rolling $z$-score,
but the residual is large. When a bucket does not yet hold enough history to trust,
the core marks it insufficient and leaves that series to the edge until the weeks
accumulate.

The two tiers come together when alerts are evaluated. Each tier writes the same
kind of finding, stamped with which tier produced it and with the series and time
it covers, so the two can be matched. If the seasonal profile already expects an
edge spike, the alert is dropped. A spike that is also off the seasonal baseline is
escalated instead. And the seasonal tier can raise an alert on its own for a slow
drift that never tripped the edge. The edge is tuned for recall and the core for
precision. We describe the seasonal tier only far enough to show how it fits with
the edge engine, which is the subject of the rest of the paper.
